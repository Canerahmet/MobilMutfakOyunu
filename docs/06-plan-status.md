- 11 September 2026: **it was not holding 30 fps on a low-end phone — and most of the loss was in five lines of settings.** A performance review counted the scene: at the largest tier 14 tables, 70 pieces of furniture, 28 badges and **46-67 figures**, each carrying 1 Animator + 2 SkinnedMeshRenderers → ~265 draw calls in the opaque pass, ~142 in the shadow pass, **~400 in total**; the practical limit for a low-end Adreno 610 is 150-200. What was fixed:
  - **URP settings:** 4x MSAA → 1, render scale 1 → 0.8, shadow map 2048² → 512, shadow distance 25 → 14 m (~~the plot is 18×9.6 m, so everything was inside the shadow map~~ — **that reasoning was in the wrong unit and was reverted the same day: URP measures this distance from the camera, and the value had switched off every shadow; see the next entry**), additional lights PerPixel → off (the fill light was opening a second pixel pass). Intermediate texture `Always` → `Auto`: that setting on its own was adding a full-screen copy every frame, **~1.2 GB/s of bandwidth, drawing nothing**.
  - **`gpuSkinning: 0` → `1`.** 134 skinned meshes were being skinned on the CPU; the bone count is 7 and `TwoBones` is already set, which means the cheapest path on the GPU side was open and was not being used.
  - **A vSync contradiction.** `BuildPlayer` writes `vSyncCount = 1`, `ProjectSetup` was writing `= 0` — and both of them to the **wrong quality level**: `ProjectSetup` to whichever is active in the editor (Ultra), while Android uses level 2. When `vSyncCount != 0`, Android **ignores** `targetFrameRate`, so on a cheap 120 Hz phone the game was targeting 120 fps — with a 30 fps floor as the target. The setting is now applied at runtime, in `GameApp.Awake`: vSync off, target **30**.
  - **67 Animators were repeating a single pose forever.** A seated customer does not move, but its Animator was being evaluated every frame: ~45 µs × 67 ≈ **3 ms/frame**, for nothing in return. The Animator is switched off once the transition finishes (`Figure.Update`) and switched back on when the pose changes; a figure returning to the pool forgets it with `Release()`.
  - **The tick budget went from 400 to 40.** `Time.deltaTime` is capped at 0.333 s, which means at the highest speed 213 ticks could pile up in a single frame and the budget allowed it — a single 22 ms frame, and a **self-feeding** spiral (long frame → bigger backlog → longer frame). On top of that, `AdvanceTasks` was scanning **1,024** job slots unconditionally every tick; the number of active jobs cannot exceed 56, and the scan is now per party.
  - **String allocation every frame.** The flow line in the top strip was doing `string.Format` + three `int` boxings even when the values had not changed at all: ~150-200 bytes/frame, **~3 MB of garbage** over an eight-minute service. The components are kept separately and the string is only produced when a number changes. (`OccupiedTables` was a loop, not a property, as well.)
  - **The save was being written twice.** When Android sends the app to the background, `OnApplicationPause(true)` **and** `OnApplicationFocus(false)` both fire; the save is ~12,500 fields and 30-60 ms on the main thread — at precisely the moment the operating system is preparing to kill the app, twice over.
  - **Sound synthesis.** The sample rate went from 44100 to **22050** (no audible difference in the click and the coin sounds, memory and time halved), and synthesis is now **lazy**: it looks at the file first. Before, all ten clips were generated unconditionally and then thrown away if the file existed.

  After the changes the tour is **36/36 clean** and 220 tests pass. No measurement could be taken (there is no device); every number in the review is a "this many objects × this much work" estimate, but that 400 draw calls + 134 CPU-skinned meshes + 4x MSAA + full resolution + a 2048² shadow map would not fit into 33 ms did not require measuring.

### Business upgrades: a deliberate deferral

`content/upgrades.json` ([13](13-data-schemas.md)) is **not a gap, it is a
feature**: purchases like a sign that raise the customer base without
adding capacity. It is a nice tycoon mechanic and it genuinely fills a
hole in the game — today every purchase sells *capacity* (equipment,
tables, storeroom) and none of them makes a single customer more valuable.

**Even so, it will not be written now.** The reason: adding a sixth spending
axis means recalibrating the balance from scratch. The realisation rate is a
**fixed point** ([32](32-equipment-and-rebalance.md)) and every new money tap
shifts that point; adding it today would mean reopening the balance of two
cuisines that are standing ready for release. The game is **playable and
balanced** without this axis.

When it gets written: after the first release goes out and it has been seen
how real players spend their 60 days — then the answer to "which purchase is
missing" is not a guess but a **measurement**.

`tools/audit_content.py` keeps these two fields (`capacityBonus`, `customerBaseBonus`)
in the queue; they are not dropped silently.

### The save migration chain: a deliberate deferral

[23-core-contract.md](23-core-contract.md) §7.6 asks for two verifications. **The interruption test has been written** and passes. **The version test** — loading an old snapshot through the migration chain and replaying it — has not been written, and that is not an omission but a decision:

The save version went from 1 to 10 within a week. Writing a migration chain today means writing ten migrations, ten of which will be thrown away. The current behaviour is **not silent**: when `Restore` sees a different version it throws an explicit error, so an old save is not corrupted, it just does not open.

**When the chain will be written:** when the first playable release goes out — that is, when somebody else's save exists for the first time. Until that moment, raising the version number is free; after it, every raise is a migration debt.
